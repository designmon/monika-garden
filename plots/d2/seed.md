# Web Greetings Layer

I want something that lets people inject themselves into any place on the web, almost like leaving a small trace anywhere. The web is supposed to be a medium for anyone, but in practice it feels like a set of separate territories owned by separate companies. You can post inside one platform, comment inside another, react inside another, but there is no shared participatory layer across the web.

I keep thinking about radio greetings: people call in, say hello to someone, dedicate a song, participate in a small competition, or say what they wish for. On some local radio, like the happy radio in Greece, people come and say what they need or wish for, and the radio looks for sponsors or people who can make it happen. That kind of public, lightweight, warm participation does not really work on the web right now.

This seed should explore how a global layer of presence could exist across websites. Maybe it is a browser extension. Maybe it is a tiny digital sofa you can place on any webpage with your customized avatar and a very short quote. Maybe it is an audio greeting, a little dedication, a wish, a “I was here,” a tiny public seat, or a sponsor-supported wish board. It should feel more like a radio greeting or public square than a comment section.

Important: it should be non-violent, short, warm, and socially safe. It should avoid becoming spam, harassment, graffiti, or another noisy social feed. The interesting design problem is: how can people leave presence, greetings, wishes, dedications, or tiny participatory traces across the web without owning the page, damaging the page, or making the page worse?

## Questions to explore

- What is the smallest trace that feels human but not invasive?
- Is the trace tied to a URL, a domain, a visual place on the page, or a moment in time?
- Can people leave greetings for strangers, friends, future visitors, or a specific person?
- Could wishes connect to sponsors or helpers without becoming gross or exploitative?
- What moderation, ephemerality, consent, and visibility rules make this feel tender instead of chaotic?

## Bloom looks like

A concept prototype for a cross-web participation layer: a tiny avatar/sofa/greeting/wish object that can be placed on any page, lets people leave a short warm trace, and makes the web feel less like separate company territories and more like a shared medium again.

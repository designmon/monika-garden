# Matchstick Release

I want a fun OS-level tool that lets me "burn" anything on my screen without actually deleting or damaging it. It should feel like taking out a matchstick or lighter, lighting it, placing it over some annoying thing, and watching that thing visually burn into ash. The real object stays exactly where it is. The tool only gives me the emotional release of seeing it burn.

Examples: a sticky note in Miro, a Slack message, a Granola note, an email in the browser, a screenshot of something that irritated me, maybe any rectangular thing I point at on the screen. I want it to touch anything, so OS-level feels ideal if technically possible. But maybe a browser extension is an easier first version for webpages, and then an OS overlay could come later.

Technically I imagine it could work by taking a screenshot of the screen area or container I hover over, creating an overlay copy above the real content, and burning only that copy. The burn could follow the outline and inside of the captured rectangle: edges curl, pixels char, little ember flecks appear, ash falls or dissolves, then the overlay disappears. Nothing underneath changes. At the end it could count how many things I burned, like a tiny relief ritual.

The important feeling is playful catharsis, not destruction. It should be safe, funny, beautiful, and temporary. A thing makes me mad, I point, click, it burns visually, and my nervous system gets a satisfying little "done" moment.

## Bloom looks like

A small prototype of a screen overlay or browser extension that lets me select a visible object, lights a match/lighter animation, burns a screenshot-copy of that object into ash, leaves the original untouched, and keeps a simple count of released burns.
